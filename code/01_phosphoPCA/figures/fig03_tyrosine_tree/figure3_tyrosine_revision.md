## 1. What changed and why

The tyrosine half of the published tree was built from inflated substrate sets.
After the fix (Option A), the serine/threonine half is unchanged (14/14 clades
identical) and the tyrosine half has a different topology (all 6 internal
groupings differ; only the whole 8-member clade is shared).

**Option A tyrosine nesting, inner → outer:**

```
(FES, FYN) → +KSYK → +FRK → +ABL2 → +STYK1 → +JAK2 → +TNK1
```

Two sentences in the published text are topology-dependent and cannot be
recovered by reordering nodes in iTOL (rotation changes only child order, never
sisterhood):

- *"FRK and FYN form the closest pair"* — the inner pair is now (FES, FYN), and
  KSYK always sits between FRK and FYN in any rendering.
- *"STYK1 fell furthest from the conventional tyrosine kinases"* — STYK1 is now
  internal; JAK2 and TNK1 are necessarily more peripheral.

**The figure file.** `screening_tree_optA_reordered_renamed.nwk` was generated by
rotating nodes of `screening_tree_optA.newick` only. Verified: 22/22 clades
identical, zero internal and zero terminal branch-length mismatches. Rendered
order is FES, FYN, KSYK, FRK, ABL2, STYK1, JAK2, TNK1, then the S/T block in
published order. Display labels: `JAK2_1`→`JAK2 - 1`, `KS6B1_active`→`KS6B1
T252D`, `KS6B1_wt`→`KS6B1`, `PLK1_active`→`PLK1 T210D`.

## 2. Manuscript draft

Reference tags map to §4. The S/T paragraphs are unchanged and are not
reproduced here.

> Within the tyrosine clade the corrected tree ordered kinases along the
> principal axis of tyrosine-kinase substrate specificity. Using
> position-specific scoring matrices from a published survey of the human
> tyrosine kinome `[R1]`, which describes tyrosine-kinase specificity as a
> continuum from acidophilic kinases to the rarer basophilic ones, we computed
> each panel member's preference for acidic versus basic residues flanking the
> phosphoacceptor. The radial order of our tyrosine clade tracked this continuum
> (Spearman ρ = −0.86, p = 0.014, invariant across all three matrix
> normalizations): the innermost kinases SYK, FYN and FES were the most
> acidophilic, with acidic:basic preference ratios of 2.0–3.3 concentrated
> N-terminal to the phosphoacceptor, and the ratio declined monotonically
> outward through FRK and ABL2 to JAK2 and TNK1. Cophenetic distances from the
> corrected tree likewise correlated with external specificity distances
> (Kendall τ = 0.52–0.60, p ≤ 1.3 × 10⁻³), whereas the uncorrected tree showed
> no correlation (τ = −0.06 to −0.10, p ≥ 0.54).
>
> The two most peripheral kinases reached that position by different routes.
> TNK1 was the only net basophilic kinase in the panel (acidic:basic 0.39), with
> lysine, arginine or histidine among its top-ranked residues at every position
> N-terminal to the phosphoacceptor — consistent with the ACK family being one
> of only two tyrosine-kinase groups reported as basophilic `[R1]`. JAK2, by
> contrast, was not basophilic but essentially charge-indifferent, its acidic
> and basic preferences both within 10% of uniform, with selectivity residing
> instead in proline and glycine N-terminal to the site and aliphatic residues
> C-terminal to it. Its peripheral position therefore reflects loss of the
> acidic-flanking determinant that organizes the rest of the clade rather than a
> reversal of it.
>
> Within the acidophilic core, the arrangement FYN–FRK–ABL2 followed a stepwise
> shift in that same determinant: acidic preference N-terminal to the
> phosphoacceptor declined while tolerance of basic residues C-terminal to it
> rose, monotonically from FYN through FRK to ABL2, placing FRK intermediate
> between the Src-family and Abl-family extremes of this axis. This is
> consistent with ancestral reconstructions indicating that the Src and Abl
> lineages acquired distinct specificities after their divergence `[ref 38]`.
> SYK sat at the acidophilic extreme and additionally showed the panel's
> strongest preference for pre-phosphorylated residues, consistent with its
> reported reliance on phosphopriming `[R1]`.
>
> These comparisons are corroboration rather than replication: the external
> matrices derive from peptide-array motif profiling whereas our tree derives
> from a selectivity screen over a finite substrate pool, in which kinases with
> few scored substrates contribute unevenly to the clustering. STYK1 is not
> represented in the external resource and is excluded from these comparisons.

### Optional sentence (descriptive only — see §5 for the caveat)

> Notably, the two most peripheral kinases are also the two whose
> accessory-domain architecture departs from the SH3–SH2–kinase module shared by
> the core — JAK2 carrying a regulatory pseudokinase (JH2) domain `[R3]` and
> TNK1 the Ack-family SAM/SH3/UBA arrangement `[R2]` — although such accessory
> domains are reported to influence cellular substrate access rather than
> intrinsic peptide selectivity `[R2]`, so this co-occurrence should be read as

def dist(a, b, kind='norm', metric='pearson'):
    x, y = M(a,kind).values.flatten(), M(b,kind).values.flatten()
    if metric == 'pearson':  return 1 - pearsonr(x, y)[0]

### Cell 6 — Tree concordance → reproduces §3.4

Parse both Newick files, compute cophenetic (patristic) distances restricted to
the 7 atlas-profiled tyrosine kinases, then Kendall τ against the atlas
distances from cell 5.

```python
def cophenetic(tree, keep):
    """sum of branch lengths on the two paths below the LCA, per leaf pair"""
    leaves = []
    def walk(n, path):
        name, ch, bl = n
        if ch is None: leaves.append((name, path + [(id(n), bl)])); return
        for c in ch: walk(c, path + [(id(n), bl)])
    walk(tree, [])
    out = {}
    for (a, pa), (b, pb) in combinations(leaves, 2):
        if a not in keep or b not in keep: continue
        k = 0
        while k < len(pa) and k < len(pb) and pa[k][0] == pb[k][0]: k += 1
        out[frozenset((a, b))] = sum(x for _, x in pa[k:]) + sum(x for _, x in pb[k:])
    return out
```
