We designed two barcoded substrate libraries totalling 3,928 ordered 15-mer
peptides (3,918 distinct sequences; Ser/Thr library n = 1,978, Tyr library
n = 1,950, with 10 sequences ordered in both). Of these, 3,312 are
sequence-diverse candidates drawn from curated phosphosite records (2,753 from
PhosphoSitePlus and 559, in the Tyr library only, from a broader animal
phosphoproteome compilation), and 616 are control windows carried over from
the kinase-substrate interaction database that KINBERT was trained on: 449
annotated as substrates of at least one of the nine targeted kinases, 165
drawn from the database's non-interacting (label-negative) pool, and 2
annotated in both directions. Because these controls were sampled from
KINBERT's own training and held-out splits, they are used here only as
wet-lab performance controls and not as an independent benchmark of the model
(see Methods, "Benchmarking against database annotations").

=============================================================================

Substrate library design. Control ("spike-in") windows were selected from the
predictions of two KINBERT checkpoints on the curated kinase-substrate
interaction database (interaction_classed_221017), separately for the nine
kinases targeted by the two libraries (Ser/Thr: Q8N5S9, P53350, Q86TW2,
P23443, Q9NR20, O43318; Tyr: P06241, P07949, Q6J9G0) and for each
phospho-amino-acid-binding-domain reporter (SH2, 14-3-3, WW, FHA). Three
classes were taken: database-positive pairs from the model's held-out test
split, database-positive pairs from its training split, and database-negative
pairs from its training split on which both checkpoints agreed (up to 30, 30
and 20 windows per kinase x reporter x phosphoacceptor cell, respectively).
Within each cell, candidate windows were greedily de-duplicated so that any
two retained windows differ by a Levenshtein distance of at least 7, and the
highest- (or, for negatives, lowest-) scoring survivors were kept. This
yielded 616 distinct control windows (465 Ser/Thr, 151 Tyr) spanning 646
distinct (kinase, window) pairs: 449 windows documented as substrates, 165
drawn from the database-negative pool, and 2 appearing in both classes.

Candidate substrates were drawn from the same curated database (all records,
irrespective of kinase) and, for the Tyr library only, from a compiled animal
phosphosite table. Each pool was greedily de-duplicated with the same
procedure, retaining windows at pairwise Levenshtein distance of at least 9
(PhosphoSitePlus-derived pool) or at least 11 (animal pool); the merged Tyr
pool was re-filtered at distance 9. Random subsamples were then combined with
the control windows and de-duplicated by sequence, giving 1,978 Ser/Thr and
1,950 Tyr oligos (3,928 rows, 3,918 distinct sequences). All windows are
15 residues with the phosphoacceptor at position 8; every Ser/Thr-library
window is Ser- or Thr-centred, and 45 Tyr-library windows are Ser/Thr-centred
because the PhosphoSitePlus pool for that library was selected by kinase
family (tyrosine kinases) rather than by phosphoacceptor identity.

Identity assignment is intrinsically ambiguous for a 15-mer: 2,069 of the
3,928 designed rows (53%) match an identical window in more than one human or
animal protein (up to 68 proteins for a single window), so the accession and
site reported in Table S13 are a deterministic representative (lowest
accession, then lowest site index) and the full match list is given in the
same row. One window, VGAPIAVSAFYAESC, could not be mapped to any accession
and is reported without identity. 32 of the 3,918 distinct designed windows
were not recovered in the screen.

Benchmarking against database annotations. The annotated pair set used in
Table S10 and Fig. [x] is NOT the designed control set. It is the
intersection of the screened (kinase, 15-mer) matrix with a combined
annotation table built from (i) the curated interaction database, excluding
records whose only evidence source was mass spectrometry, yeast two-hybrid,
PhosphoNetworks, DeepKinZero, Phospho.ELM or KinasePhos, and (ii) the
label-negative rows of KINBERT v1's held-out database test split, which are
randomly sampled decoy pairs rather than experimentally demonstrated
non-substrates. Three kinase constructs (KS6B1_HUMAN_active, KS6B1_HUMAN_wt,
PLK1_HUMAN_active) were excluded. This gives 403 annotated rows over 385
distinct (kinase, 15-mer) pairs: 329 rows annotated positive and 74 annotated
negative.

Because both the annotation table and KINBERT's training corpus derive from
the same curated database, this benchmark is not independent of the model.
Matching on the exact (kinase catalytic-domain sequence, 15-mer) key used by
the training files, 764 of the 29,799 screened pairs (2.6%) occur somewhere in
KINBERT's public-data corpus, and 310 of the 385 benchmark pairs (80.5%) do:
205 in the training split, 29 in validation, 38 in test and 38 in the
secondary test split. A further 127 of the 385 (33%) are in the training split
of the screen-derived phospho-PCA fine-tuning set, so 338 of 385 (88%) were
seen on the training side of one or the other model. Table S10 should
therefore be read as a concordance measurement between the screen and the
curated database, not as a held-out evaluation of KINBERT; the model's
held-out performance is reported separately on splits that exclude these
pairs.
